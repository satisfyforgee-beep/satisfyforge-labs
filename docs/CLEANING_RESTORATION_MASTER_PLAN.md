# Cleaning & Restoration Master Plan — Middletown, Ohio

Updated 2026-10-13. This is the canonical business-only plan for the cleaning/restoration workstream. It intentionally excludes household finances, personal account balances, tax identifiers, bank information and other sensitive owner data.

## Operating goal

Build a serious cleaning and restoration company from Middletown, Ohio, serving roughly the Cincinnati–Dayton corridor and nearby communities. Start with services that can be sold and fulfilled safely with low fixed overhead, then expand into higher-ticket restoration and specialty work as insurance, training, equipment, staffing and demand justify it.

Long-term service families:
- residential/commercial carpet and upholstery cleaning
- tile/grout and hard-surface cleaning
- exterior washing / pressure washing where appropriate
- concrete / garage-floor cleaning
- auto/interior detailing
- ventilation/duct cleaning within the legal/service boundary
- water-damage mitigation and structural drying
- odor / smoke / fire-cleaning support
- mold/microbial remediation after proper training, controls and insurance
- contents cleaning / restoration

Do not advertise or perform licensed HVAC repair, plumbing, electrical, refrigeration, hydronics, lead abatement, asbestos abatement or other regulated trade work merely because the company performs cleaning/restoration. Refer or subcontract regulated work to appropriately licensed/certified firms.

## Zero-spend launch rule

The owner does not front cash for speculative equipment.

Before buying a Butler truckmount, van, trailer, drying fleet or specialty restoration inventory, first prove demand with one or more of these structures:
1. pre-sold jobs with a written scope and customer payment sufficient to cover the specific job's lawful rental/subcontract cost;
2. subcontract/partner work where an established insured firm supplies specialty equipment;
3. equipment rental only after a booked job covers the rental, consumables, transport, insurance requirements and margin;
4. used-equipment opportunity funded by business revenue, legitimate financing approved by the owner, or an outside assistance program—not hidden personal spending.

Never take a job that cannot be fulfilled safely, legally and insured merely to generate cash.

## Phase 1 — low-capital cleaning services

Prioritize services that can become customer-funded without a truckmount fleet:

### Carpet / upholstery cleaning
Preferred long-term system: Butler truckmount or another proven professional platform after demand and funding justify it.

Before owning a truckmount:
- use an insured subcontract partner or lawful rental model for larger jobs;
- avoid presenting rented/subcontracted equipment as owned;
- build the inspection, chemistry, spotting, fiber-identification and customer-expectation process first.

IICRC's current Carpet Cleaning Technician (CCT) certification has no prerequisite and covers pre-inspection, fiber identification, cleaning chemistry/equipment and professional residential/light-commercial carpet cleaning. It is a strong early credential when funding permits.

### Tile/grout / hard surface
Sell only methods appropriate for the material and finish. Inspection must identify loose grout, damaged sealers/coatings, natural stone, pre-existing cracking and moisture issues before pressure/chemistry is selected.

### Exterior washing
Ohio treats cleaning building exteriors—including power washing siding, roofs, chimneys and gutters—as building-maintenance/janitorial cleaning for sales-tax purposes when the statutory threshold applies. Distinguish cleaning from repair, coating removal and construction work.

### Auto/interior detailing
Ohio lists car cleaning/waxing/polishing as taxable. Keep this revenue/category separate enough in bookkeeping to collect/remit correctly.

## Phase 2 — water mitigation / structural drying

Do not enter insurance-style mitigation as simply 'more carpet cleaning.' Build the operational system first:
- emergency intake and loss category/source questions
- moisture inspection and documentation
- extraction / controlled demolition decision process
- equipment placement map
- daily monitoring / psychrometric documentation
- customer/adjuster communication log
- work authorization and scope change controls
- drying goal / completion documentation
- photo evidence before, during and after
- contamination/PPE decision tree

Training path:
1. **IICRC WRT — Water Restoration Technician**
2. **IICRC ASD — Applied Structural Drying** after WRT
3. HST/OCT/CPT/FSRT as the service mix expands

IICRC states ASD requires WRT and focuses on drying water-damaged structures safely and efficiently.

Start with clean-water/simpler mitigation only when training, insurance and equipment are appropriate. Sewage, gross contamination, microbial growth, regulated materials and significant demolition require additional controls and may exceed an early-stage company's safe scope.

## Phase 3 — mold / microbial remediation

Do not market mold remediation based only on general restoration experience.

IICRC's AMRT certification covers mold and sewage remediation and currently requires WRT first. AMRT is an appropriate technical progression before offering dedicated microbial-remediation work.

Operational requirements before taking microbial jobs should include:
- written hazard/PPE assessment
- containment / pressure-control plan when applicable
- respirator program/fit testing where respiratory protection is required
- material-removal and bagging process
- HEPA filtration/vacuum procedures
- moisture-source correction/referral boundary
- independent testing/IEP relationship where appropriate
- documentation and post-remediation criteria

OSHA requires employers to assess workplace hazards and document PPE assessments when PPE is required. Mold/water-damage work can involve respiratory, chemical, sharp-object, biological and structural hazards.

Do not perform asbestos abatement, lead abatement or another regulated environmental activity without the applicable credentials and legal authority.

## Lead-safe renovation boundary

Restoration can become renovation when painted surfaces are disturbed.

EPA's Renovation, Repair and Painting (RRP) rule generally requires firm and renovator certification when paid work disturbs paint in pre-1978 housing or child-occupied facilities beyond the rule's thresholds. Ohio is not listed among the states operating its own RRP program in lieu of EPA, so the federal EPA firm-certification route is relevant for covered Ohio work.

Until certified, scope jobs to avoid covered paint disturbance or use an appropriately certified partner. Never advertise covered renovation work that requires certification before the firm is certified.

## Ohio business / tax compliance

### Entity

An Ohio LLC is the preferred structure to evaluate for liability separation and business credibility, but the owner should choose the final tax/entity setup with an accountant/attorney if needed. Ohio Secretary of State filings are handled through Ohio Business Central / Secretary of State Business Services.

### Sales tax

Ohio currently treats many planned services as taxable building-maintenance/janitorial services once the statutory $5,000 calendar-year sales threshold is reached. The state's examples include:
- carpet and upholstery cleaning
- interior/exterior building cleaning
- window cleaning
- floor washing/waxing
- exterior power washing
- ventilating-system / duct cleaning
- disinfecting and deodorizing

Ohio also lists car cleaning/waxing/polishing as taxable.

Once the company is making taxable sales requiring collection, obtain the appropriate Ohio vendor's license and use OH|TAX eServices for sales-tax registration/filing. Ohio's current vendor-license fee is $50. Do not wait until bookkeeping is a mess to separate taxable and non-taxable lines.

Mixed taxable/nontaxable work should be itemized clearly; Ohio warns that bundling without proper separation can make the entire charge taxable in some situations.

### Local / trade licensing

Ohio's Construction Industry Licensing Board lists statewide contractor licenses for electrical, HVAC, plumbing, hydronics and refrigeration. Cleaning/restoration itself is not a substitute for those trade licenses. Check city/local requirements and the exact scope before adding work that crosses into construction or a regulated trade.

## Insurance checklist

Get actual quotes before launch; do not assume a general-liability policy covers restoration pollution/mold losses.

Ask the broker specifically about:
- commercial general liability
- commercial auto / hired and non-owned auto as applicable
- inland marine / tools and equipment
- workers' compensation when required
- contractor pollution liability / environmental coverage before mold/sewage/restoration exposure
- errors/omissions if inspection/consulting services are sold
- care, custody and control / bailee exposure for customer contents
- water-damage exclusions
- mold/fungi/bacteria exclusions
- subcontractor requirements / certificates of insurance

Never advertise 'fully insured' until the actual active policies match the work being offered.

## Safety system

Before field work, create:
- SDS library for every chemical
- labeled secondary-container procedure
- PPE matrix by task
- written hazard-assessment process
- respirator program when respirators are required
- ladder / electrical / slip-trip / confined-space boundaries as relevant
- bloodborne-pathogen plan only if work can reasonably expose employees to blood/OPIM
- heat/cold stress process
- incident / near-miss form
- stop-work authority for unknown asbestos/lead/structural/electrical hazards

Do not mix bleach with ammonia-containing cleaners. Use manufacturer labels/SDS rather than internet chemistry recipes as the operating standard.

## Job economics model

Each quote must cover:
1. labor hours
2. travel/vehicle cost
3. chemicals/consumables
4. equipment rental/depreciation
5. disposal
6. subcontractors
7. insurance/admin allocation
8. sales tax where applicable
9. payment-processing cost
10. contingency/rework risk
11. profit

For restoration, add equipment-days and daily monitoring/documentation labor. Never use revenue alone as a measure of a 'good job.'

## Customer-funded startup model

Where lawful and appropriate:
- small cleaning: collect payment at completion or a clearly disclosed booking policy;
- custom/specialty jobs: collect a deposit only after the business can actually fulfill the job, with written cancellation/refund terms;
- jobs requiring rentals/subcontractors: quote those costs into the job before acceptance;
- restoration/insurance: use clear work authorizations and do not tell customers the insurer 'will pay' unless coverage/payment is confirmed by the responsible party.
