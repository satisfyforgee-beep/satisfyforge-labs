# Short-Form Growth Playbook — 2026-10-13

## Operating rule
Study winning videos aggressively, reproduce the **mechanics that make them work**, and rebuild the execution from scratch.

We can match:
- hook type;
- pacing;
- reveal timing;
- narrative shape;
- shot density;
- caption rhythm;
- payoff structure;
- comment prompt;
- series structure;
- trend participation;
- thumbnail/cover readability;
- posting experiments.

We should not simply repost a creator's footage, script, voice, graphics or story with trivial edits. This is not just an IP concern: both YouTube and Facebook are explicitly increasing distribution for original content and reducing reach for low-value reposts/minor edits.

## What the platforms are signaling right now
### YouTube Shorts
YouTube says Shorts ranking is driven by whether viewers choose to watch, whether they stay, average view duration, average percentage viewed, engagement and satisfaction. Current Shorts analytics explicitly expose **viewed vs swiped away**. In October 2026, YouTube also announced stronger prioritization of original Shorts and reduced distribution for channels mainly re-uploading other creators without substantial added value.

Translation for SatisfyForge:
- first frame must make the subject obvious;
- first sentence must create a reason not to swipe;
- eliminate setup that can be inferred visually;
- move the strongest moment earlier;
- turn retention spikes into new videos/series;
- remake winning *ideas* with original footage, narration and payoff.

### TikTok
TikTok's 2026 trend guidance emphasizes real process, behind-the-scenes moments, community participation, comments as a creative surface, curiosity-led discovery and culturally fluent responses. TikTok's current Creative Center also exposes live US trending topics/videos for ongoing research.

Translation:
- less polished corporate voice, more real process;
- show the build/test/failure rather than just the finished asset;
- replies and comments become future video prompts;
- connect the first hook to something viewers are already curious about;
- publish multiple angles on the same winning concept rather than constantly abandoning a format.

### Facebook Reels
Meta's 2026 guidance says original content is being rewarded with more reach and monetization while duplicative content and minor edits to other creators' posts are deprioritized. Adding borders, captions or speed changes alone is specifically not enough.

Translation:
- Facebook versions should be natively edited from our source project;
- add real narration, analysis, demonstration or a new storyline;
- do not use a repost pipeline as the core business model.

## The SatisfyForge viral structure
Every short should be designed around this sequence unless the data proves another format wins:

**1. Pattern interrupt (0-1.5s)**
Show the result, danger, surprise, contradiction or strongest visual first.

**2. Open loop (1-4s)**
Make the viewer need an answer.

**3. Escalation (4-15s)**
Each beat changes the situation. No redundant exposition.

**4. Payoff (before attention decays)**
Deliver the promised reveal; do not withhold it artificially.

**5. Second reward / twist**
Give one extra fact, failure, reaction or consequence.

**6. Loop or conversation trigger**
End in a way that creates rewatch, debate, prediction or a natural Part 2.

## Hook families to continuously test
Use original wording every time, but repeat the successful structure.

- **Result first:** “This actually worked way better than it should have.”
- **Failure first:** “I broke the entire thing in less than 30 seconds.”
- **Constraint:** “I tried building this without spending a dollar.”
- **Comparison:** “The cheap version beat the expensive one.”
- **Challenge:** “Can this Roblox game keep me playing for 10 minutes?”
- **Curiosity:** “There is one reason this game has so many players.”
- **Escalation:** “Every round gets worse.”
- **Unexpected payoff:** show the rare drop/result before explaining how it happened.
- **Comment-driven:** “You told me to add this, so I did.”
- **Proof:** “I tested the thing everyone keeps claiming works.”

## SatisfyForge repeatable series
### Roblox
1. **Building the game players won't leave** — one mechanic per episode.
2. **I copied the winning mechanic, not the game** — compare a market pattern, then show our original version.
3. **Can this mechanic survive a real player test?** — private client footage, bug, fix, retest.
4. **Rare rescue moments** — shortest possible setup, visual rarity payoff.
5. **I let the comments change the game** — turn top comments into actual updates.

### Tools / business / AI
1. **I replaced a paid tool for $0**.
2. **I made AI do the boring part**.
3. **This automation failed — here is the fix**.
4. **Can this make its first dollar?** — honest experiment, no fabricated revenue.
5. **One feature in 30 seconds** — before/after demo.

### History / entertainment / faceless
1. **The part nobody tells you first**.
2. **The moment everything went wrong**.
3. **Three facts, but the last one changes the story**.
4. **Visual reconstruction + narration** using original/licensed/generated visuals.
5. **Comment correction / myth check** as a follow-up.

## Editing rules
- Cut every pause that does not add tension.
- Start on motion, a face/reaction, a result, or a visually legible object.
- Make captions readable without covering the focal action.
- Use sound effects to reinforce transitions/payoffs, not every half-second.
- Never keep an intro/logo before the hook.
- Default to one idea per short.
- Use zoom/crop/reframe only when it directs attention.
- If a reveal is the reason to watch, show evidence immediately after the claim.
- Build endings that can loop cleanly when appropriate.

## Testing system
Do not judge a format from one upload. For a promising concept, produce a small controlled family.

Suggested test cell:
- 3 hooks for the same core video;
- 2 lengths when practical;
- 2 caption/title framings;
- same core payoff.

Track per platform:
- viewed vs swiped away / initial hold where available;
- average view duration;
- average percentage viewed;
- rewatches/retention spikes;
- shares;
- saves;
- comments per 1,000 views;
- profile/channel visits;
- follows/subscribers per 1,000 views;
- outbound clicks/revenue only when relevant.

A high-view video with poor follows/clicks may be entertainment-only; that is fine if its job is reach. Do not force every video to sell.

## Clone-the-mechanic workflow
When we find a breakout video:

1. Record the hook in abstract terms, not its exact wording.
2. Record the first visual and why it stops scrolling.
3. Mark every major beat by timestamp.
4. Identify where the viewer gets new information.
5. Identify the payoff and secondary payoff.
6. Identify the comment/share trigger.
7. Rebuild the same beat map around a SatisfyForge subject.
8. Use our own footage, generated visuals, narration, captions and ending.
9. Test at least two different opening executions.
10. Feed performance back into the next batch.

Example abstraction:
- competitor: “I bought the cheapest ___ and this happened”
- pattern: low-cost constraint + immediate visual proof + escalating test + surprising result
- our version: “I tried building a Roblox retention feature for $0 and this is what happened.”

That keeps the proven psychological structure without making a low-value copy.

## Cross-platform adaptation
Do not blindly upload the identical export everywhere.

- **TikTok:** lean hardest into comments, cultural language, process and native trend participation.
- **YouTube Shorts:** optimize first for chosen-to-view, hold, completion and repeatable topic clusters.
- **Instagram Reels:** favor clean visual storytelling, shareability and strong cover/frame selection; build from original master footage.
- **Facebook Reels:** use the original source and meaningful transformation; avoid minor-edited repost behavior because Meta explicitly deprioritizes it.

One master project can feed all platforms, but each version should be intentionally packaged.

## Content-production priority
For the next SatisfyForge batch, prioritize proof over generic AI filler:
1. real Snackquake gameplay/test footage;
2. before/after game changes;
3. failures and fixes;
4. unusual automation outcomes;
5. visual experiments with clear results;
6. comment-driven revisions.

The Roblox project itself can become a content engine: every meaningful feature, bug, rare event, test and player reaction is potential short-form material.
