Contractor Lead Rescue Kit

Follow-up templates for quote requests, missed calls, old estimates, no-shows, reviews, repeat business and referrals—built for local service companies where one recovered job can pay for the entire system.

Rule #1: never manufacture urgency. Replace placeholders with real information and stop when someone says no or opts out.
CORE SEQUENCE

4-message quote recovery

Use this for a qualified inquiry or estimate that has gone quiet.

DAY 0 — FAST RESPONSE
Hi [FIRST NAME] — this is [NAME] with [BUSINESS NAME]. Thanks for reaching out about [SERVICE]. I can help you figure out the next step. Are you mainly trying to solve [COMMON PROBLEM A], [COMMON PROBLEM B], or something else?
DAY 2 — HELPFUL CHECK-IN
Hi [FIRST NAME], just checking back on [SERVICE]. No pressure if you’re still comparing options. If there’s a question about price, timing, process, or what you actually need, send it over and I’ll give you a straight answer.
DAY 5 — CLEAR NEXT STEP
Quick follow-up on [SERVICE]: we currently have [REAL AVAILABILITY / REAL NEXT STEP]. If you want, I can hold the next step long enough to confirm whether the timing works for you.
DAY 10 — CLOSE THE LOOP
Last check-in from me so I don’t keep bugging you. If you still need help with [SERVICE], reply here anytime and I’ll pick it back up. If you already handled it, no worries at all.

Missed-call recovery

Hi — this is [NAME] from [BUSINESS NAME]. Sorry we missed your call. What can we help you with today?
If it’s easier, text me the service you need + your ZIP code and I can tell you the quickest next step.

No-show / reschedule

Hi [FIRST NAME], looks like today’s [ESTIMATE/APPOINTMENT] didn’t work out. No problem. Want me to send two new times so we can get it rescheduled?
I’m closing out today’s schedule. If you still want help with [SERVICE], reply with a day that usually works and we’ll see what we can do.

Old estimate reactivation

Hi [FIRST NAME], [NAME] from [BUSINESS NAME] here. You had asked us about [SERVICE] a while back. Did you end up getting that project handled, or is it still on your list?
We’re planning our [MONTH/SEASON] schedule and I saw your old [SERVICE] estimate. If the project is still active, I can update the next step rather than making you start over.
If you postponed [SERVICE] because of budget or timing, I can also tell you what parts are most urgent vs. what can reasonably wait.

Review request

Thanks again for trusting [BUSINESS NAME] with [SERVICE]. If everything looks good, would you mind leaving an honest review? It helps local customers know what to expect from us. [REVIEW LINK]
Before I send the review link—was there anything about the job you wish we had done differently? I’d rather hear it directly and make it right if we can.

Referral request

Glad we could help with [SERVICE]. If a neighbor, friend, or family member runs into the same problem, feel free to send them our info. We’ll take care of them the same way.
We have [REAL CAPACITY/SEASONAL WINDOW] coming up. If you know somebody who has been putting off [SERVICE], I’m happy to answer their questions without any pressure.

Past-customer repeat business

Hi [FIRST NAME], it’s [NAME] from [BUSINESS NAME]. We helped you with [PAST SERVICE] [TIMEFRAME] ago. I’m doing a quick customer check-in—everything still holding up the way it should?
We’re heading into [SEASON]. If you want, I can send you a quick maintenance checklist for [RELATED AREA] so you know what to watch before it becomes expensive.
Since we already know the property/job history, I wanted to give you first shot at [REAL SEASONAL SERVICE / MAINTENANCE WINDOW] before we fill the schedule.

Price objection responses

“YOUR PRICE IS TOO HIGH.”
Totally fair to compare. I’d make sure you’re comparing the same scope, materials, warranty and cleanup—not just the bottom-line number. If you send me what you’re comparing, I can explain where our quote differs without trashing the other company.
“I NEED TO THINK ABOUT IT.”
Absolutely. What part are you mainly thinking through—price, timing, whether the work is necessary, or choosing between companies? If I know that, I can give you the information you actually need and then leave you alone to decide.
“CAN YOU DO IT CHEAPER?”
I don’t want to cut something important just to hit a number. If budget is the issue, I can show you whether there’s a smaller scope or phased option that still solves the main problem.

Seasonal offer frameworks

Deadline offer: Book [SERVICE] before [REAL DATE/WEATHER WINDOW] and include [LOW-COST BONUS].
Bundle offer: Combine [SERVICE A] + [SERVICE B] while the crew/equipment is already there.
Neighborhood offer: If two nearby homes book the same route/day, pass along part of the saved travel/setup cost.
Priority offer: Existing customers get first access to [LIMITED REAL CAPACITY] before the public schedule fills.

Simple lead tracker

Every lead should end in one of five buckets so “follow-up” stops being random:

BOOKED — appointment/job scheduled.
INTERESTED — active conversation, next action has a date.
LATER — legitimate future timing; set a reminder.
NO — declined or opted out; stop.
WRONG FIT — service/area/budget not appropriate.

Numbers to track weekly

New qualified leads Speed-to-first-response Leads receiving 2+ follow-ups Replies Appointments / estimates booked Jobs sold Booked revenue Estimated gross profit Reviews received Repeat / referral jobs

The purpose of this kit is not to send more messages. It is to recover profitable opportunities without making customers feel chased.
