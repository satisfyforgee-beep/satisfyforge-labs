What I'm working toward

My name is Dylan Maloney. I'm 27, a father of two from Middletown, Ohio, and I work full-time as a material handler. I've often worked six days a week or picked up overtime when available. My family pays about $1,400 a month for a two-bedroom rental. After rent, utilities, food, vehicle costs, and other responsibilities, saving enough to start a business is difficult.

I'm not claiming a sudden emergency or asking anyone to replace my paycheck. I'm asking for an opportunity to put skills I've already developed to work for myself, gradually and responsibly, while continuing to provide for my family.

I've worked in professional carpet cleaning and property restoration, including with Teasdale Fenton and SERVPRO. My experience includes carpet and upholstery cleaning and work related to water-damage and restoration cleanup. I want to begin with manageable carpet and upholstery jobs, then add services only as I obtain the right equipment, insurance, and capacity.

My long-term goal is a well-equipped cleaning and restoration company, potentially including a Butler truckmount system. The initial $5,000 will not buy a complete truckmount van or launch a full restoration operation. The first milestone is much smaller: establish a properly insured setup, rent or obtain suitable portable equipment, and work toward the first paying customers without abandoning my current job.

I've also explored digital-business projects through SatisfyForge, but the immediate startup plan described here centers on carpet and upholstery cleaning and honest, hands-on service work.

No donation is expected. Even $5 is appreciated if someone can spare it, and I understand completely if they can't. — Dylan Maloney

The first practical milestone

1. Prepare legally and safely

Confirm registration, insurance coverage, service scope, and a way to transport the right equipment.

2. Source suitable equipment

Compare professional rental options, a used portable extractor, cleaning chemicals, attachments, and basic protective supplies.

3. Take on manageable jobs

Begin with carpet and upholstery work appropriate to the equipment and coverage obtained. Document results and reinvest earnings before considering a truckmount or advanced restoration services.

This is a proposed plan, not a claim that the business is already operating, insured, or earning revenue. Equipment rental, insurance, and transportation details still require quotes and verification.
